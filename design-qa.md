## Comparison Review

- Typography: Plus Jakarta Sans display hierarchy, Inter body copy, and JetBrains Mono technical labels match the concept's editorial/technical contrast. Headline scale and fold position were corrected during pass two.
- Layout and spacing: header, split hero, proof block, scroll cue, and first pillar section align with the selected concept. Service details now use a concise proposition, supporting paragraph, and balanced two-column composition instead of treating the full summary as a headline. No horizontal overflow was detected at 1536, 1440, 1190, or 390 CSS pixels.
- Color and surfaces: official Novyrix orange, warm white, charcoal, and restrained line tokens are consistent across both themes. The generated topography assets are theme-specific rather than approximated with CSS art.
- Imagery and icons: all hero imagery uses generated raster assets sized for the slot. Phosphor icons replaced temporary CSS signal marks and maintain one stroke family throughout.
- Content: service, proof, pricing, inquiry, work, about, and blog copy is coherent with the PRD. Page-specific service explanations, FAQs, metadata, canonical URLs, and structured data use natural search language without keyword repetition. The developing vulnerability assessment offer is explicitly marked.
- Responsive behavior: the 390px hero, proof stack, service detail, FAQ stack, header, and mobile navigation remain readable without clipping or overlap. The reported service proposition is two lines at 1190px and three lines at 390px. Practical tap targets are at least 46px.
- Accessibility: semantic navigation, headings, fieldsets, labels, button states, focus-visible treatment, decorative image alt handling, skip navigation, inert closed menus, Escape focus return, and reduced-motion fallbacks are present. The final local Lighthouse accessibility score is 100.
- Performance: the H1 paints immediately, reveal motion uses server-rendered view-timeline CSS, Lenis loads only on fine-pointer desktop devices after startup, and telemetry loads only on Vercel. The final throttled local mobile baseline is 83 performance with 1.29s FCP, 2.70s LCP, 532ms TBT, and zero CLS.
- Private payment: the quieter settlement layout keeps the public design language but prioritizes invoice reference, organization, due state, line items, total, and provider choice. The amount remains prominent without turning the page into a generic checkout card.
- Operations Console: the orange control strip, technical grid, broad editorial masthead, compact workflow panels, and restrained status colors distinguish the private surface without breaking the public brand system. Static titles were shortened after the first capture, and the public sticky header was removed because it obscured workflow headings and competed with operational navigation.
- Client portal: the second pass removes the page-wide grid that competed with content and replaces it with a bounded architectural signal behind a shorter editorial masthead. The shared shell now uses compact active navigation and the existing theme control. Invoices read as structured financial records rather than generic white cards, while Paystack and BTCPay use the same component geometry and hierarchy with orange reserved for the recommended route. Dedicated payment captures found and resolved unequal mobile action heights and a clipped compact invoice amount.
- Field notes: the index uses a ledger-like numbered feature and working archive instead of generic editorial cards. Article pages separate a compact masthead, sticky desktop contents, large opening argument, controlled body measure, operational checkpoints, primary references, and related service paths. Screenshot review reduced the mobile opening scale after the first capture while retaining the stronger desktop editorial rhythm. Light and dark versions preserve the same hierarchy without background clashes or title clipping.
